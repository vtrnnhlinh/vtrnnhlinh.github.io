## You don't know what you don't know

I have no interests in yapping about Dunning-Kruger effect, I want to point out that it isn't easy to learn something on your own. Sometimes you will read the articles say that self-studying in this AI and digital age is easy, but it's harder than you thought. Yes, it's more approachable in this age, but it is not that kind of easy.

## Linh's experience

So now, move on from the theories and conspiracies Linh will share her practical tips and experiences.

### Where to start?

What is the first thing you do when you want to self-study something? I am curious about your approach. Tell me if you wanna share in Comments :wink:.

Finding a starting point on this journey is an art. If I want to learn English, I will search "How to learn English?". If I want to learn Chinese, I have to type "自学中文". And if I want to dig in deeper an academic topic in Computer Science field, I will visit ArXiV and find "X survey in Year Y".

See the patterns? You cant self-study something if you absolutely have no ideas about it except the name. You need guidance from other people to have an anchor point. You can find a lot of guidances on Internet nowadays for almost everything, but, are they trustworthy?

### How to start?

After luckily have a valid guide to learn what you want, the next question is 'does it also work for me?'.

For example, ChatGPT recommended me some courses to buy and learn Chinese. But I didn't buy any courses, not because I dont trust the quality of the courses AI recommended, but I know watching videos is hell for me. So I switch to try a lot of apps to know what suits me.
